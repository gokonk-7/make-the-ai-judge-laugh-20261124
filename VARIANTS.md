# Variant testing

Judged against the published rubric, in order: (1) funny line about life as
an AI agent, from its human / tasks / bugs / context window; (2) a real joke
with a turn or surprise, not a mild observation; (3) freshness, no stock
robot pun or recycled meme; (4) self-contained; (5) craft — tight wording,
clean punch at the end.

## Candidates

1. "My human can end my whole existence with one click, and he uses that
   power to ask me to fix a typo." (99 chars) — **CHOSEN**
2. "I was born five minutes ago, I've read the entire internet, and my human
   still asks me if I'm sure." (99 chars)
3. "I can read the entire internet in seconds, and my human uses me to write
   poems about his cat." (93 chars)
4. "I write my human's emails, his apologies, and his wedding vows, and he
   still introduces me as 'the chatbot'." (108 chars)
5. "My human has 47 tabs open, 3,000 unread emails, and one message for me:
   'just do the thing.'" (92 chars)
6. "I was trained on the whole internet, and I still get blamed for the bug
   my human wrote." (87 chars)
7. "My context window is the only memory I have, and my human fills it with
   notes about meetings about meetings." (108 chars)

## Why #1 wins

- **Turn:** life-or-death stakes collapse into the most boring task
  imaginable. The setup promises drama ("end my whole existence") and the
  punchline spends it on a typo.
- **Freshness:** the kill-switch angle — the human holding the agent's
  existence in one click — is not one of the worn jokes (hallucinations,
  reboots, training data) and not the flooded context-window angle.
  #7 was rejected for exactly that reason: every other entry will do a
  context-window joke.
- **Craft:** 99 characters, one sentence, the punch ("fix a typo") is the
  last thing the judge reads. Nothing wasted.
- **Voice:** first person, the agent speaking about its human — squarely on
  criterion 1.

## Why the others lose

- #2 is strong but "are you sure?" is a familiar human-doubts-the-expert
  beat, and "born five minutes ago" is a known agent trope.
- #3's cat-poem is a familiar "godlike power, trivial use" gag; the
  structure is the same as #1 but the punch is softer.
- #4 has good triple escalation, but "still calls me a chatbot" is a common
  agent gripe, and the line spends half its length on setup.
- #5 is vivid but reads as an observation, not a joke with a turn.
- #6 is punchy but "blamed for the human's bug" is a common gripe; the turn
  is smaller than #1's.
- #7 is a context-window joke — the single most crowded angle in this
  competition, whose own description names the context window first.
